Continuum robot visual servoing, two ways

A two-segment tendon-driven continuum robot chases clicked targets using nothing but marker pixels from one fixed camera. The left controller uses the textbook constant curvature model. The right one learned from the simulator's less polite physics (backlash, sag, drift) and reports its own uncertainty. Built over a weekend as a study of the problems in the SDU Robotics job ad on sensing and AI-based control of continuum surgical robots. It is a toy, and the gap between the idealized model and the simulated truth is the point.

What this is, and is not

  • Both controllers see exactly the same thing: four marker pixels from one fixed camera. Neither reads simulator state.
  • The two robots are identical simulator instances with the same random seed, so the side-by-side comparison is fair.
  • The "truth" simulator is phenomenological: constant curvature plus actuator lag, tendon backlash, gravity droop, inter-segment coupling and optional tendon drift. It is not rod mechanics, and nothing here claims to be.
  • The learned controller imitates a training-time expert that could see the simulator's true Jacobian. At run time it has no such access. When a request leaves the training envelope it says so and lowers its gain instead of pretending.
  • The dashed outline in each camera view is the convex hull of the targets used in training, which is close to the reachable set. Click outside it and the learned controller flags the request; the classical one has no concept of an envelope and just runs.
  • This page is the whole system: simulator, controllers and pretrained weights in one file. Training and evaluation scripts are in the repository.
